About this site

This is a small, plain-English guide to the public transport options in Basel, Switzerland. It exists because, while the operators each do a great job for their own service, no single page lays out the trade-offs between them in one place — especially for someone who has just arrived in the city.

What this site is

  • Mode-by-mode descriptions of trams, buses, the Rhine ferries, trains and shared bikes.
  • Honest comparisons — when each mode is the right choice, and when it isn't.
  • Outbound links to the official operators for tickets, timetables, fares and live information.

What this site is not

  • Not a journey planner. Use SBB, BVB or your favourite map app — they're vastly better at this.
  • Not a place to buy tickets. Tickets must be bought from the operator (machine, app or counter).
  • Not affiliated with any operator. No money changes hands; no operator has reviewed or endorsed the content.
  • Not real-time. If a tram line is suspended for construction this morning, this site won't tell you. The BVB and SBB apps will.

Sources

Where specific facts (line numbers, ferry names, fare zones) appear, the source of truth is always the relevant operator. The main ones:

  • BVB — Basler Verkehrs-Betriebe City trams and buses in Basel.
  • BLT — Baselland Transport Trams and buses for the Basel-Landschaft canton, including the well-known cross-border tram lines.
  • TNW — Tarifverbund Nordwestschweiz The shared fare network covering BVB, BLT, S-Bahn and more.
  • SBB CFF FFS Swiss Federal Railways — S-Bahn and long-distance trains.
  • Fähri-Verein Basel The four reaction ferries that cross the Rhine.
  • PubliBike Switzerland-wide bike-share, with stations across Basel.
  • Pick-e-Bike Free-floating e-bike sharing in Basel.
