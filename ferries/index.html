Rhine ferries

Four small wooden ferries cross the Rhine using only the river's current. They are not the fastest way to get anywhere, and that's precisely the point.

What it is

Basel's Fähren are reaction ferries: a cable spans the Rhine high above the water, the boat hangs from a sliding pulley, and the ferry-master angles the rudder so that the current itself pushes the boat across. No engine, no fuel, no noise — just the sound of the river.

Four ferries cross the Rhine within Basel. Each has its own character and its own crossing point, and each is operated by a small foundation rather than the city transit authority.

The four ferries

  • Klingental ("Vogel Gryff")Between Klingental (Kleinbasel) and the Mittlere Brücke area
  • Münster ("Leu")Below Münsterhügel — the most central and popular crossing
  • St. Alban ("Wild Maa")Quieter, near the St. Alban paper mill museum
  • St. Johann ("Ueli")Furthest downstream, near the Novartis campus

When to take a ferry

  • The bridges are crowded and you have ten minutes to spare.
  • You want the best low-angle view of Basel from the water.
  • You're showing the city to a visitor and want one definitively local moment.
  • You're heading to one of the riverside Buvettes (summer bars on the Kleinbasel bank).

When something else is better

  • It's the middle of winter or the river is in flood — services pause.
  • You're in a hurry — walk the bridge, it's faster.
  • You need step-free boarding — accessibility varies by ferry.

Practicalities

  • TicketsCash on board (small fare). TNW tickets do not apply.
  • Operating seasonRoughly March–October daily; reduced or paused otherwise.
  • Crossing time3–5 minutes
  • BikesAllowed on most ferries for a small extra fare
High water, ice or strong current can suspend service without warning. If a ferry is your only way across, have a backup.
