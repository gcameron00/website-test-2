Buses

Buses do the work the trams can't. They climb the hills, reach the outer neighbourhoods, run to the airport and keep the city moving late on Friday and Saturday nights.

What it is

Bus services in Basel are run by the same operators as the trams — BVB in the city and BLT in the surrounding canton — alongside PostBus (Postauto) for some regional routes. Trolleybuses (electric, overhead-wire) and diesel/hybrid buses are both in service.

All of these accept the same TNW ticket as the trams, so a single zone-based purchase covers a multi-leg journey that mixes the two.

When to take a bus

  • Going to EuroAirport (BSL/MLH/EAP) — bus 50 from Basel SBB station.
  • Up the hill to neighbourhoods like Bruderholz or out to St. Chrischona.
  • Late nights on weekends — the night network takes over after the trams stop.
  • Suburbs and villages without a tram line.

When something else is better

  • Within the city core and during the day, the tram is usually faster and more frequent.
  • Going to Zürich or further afield — take a train.
  • Crossing the Rhine for the joy of it — take a ferry.

Notable routes

  • Bus 50Basel SBB ↔ EuroAirport (Switzerland exit)
  • Night networkFriday and Saturday nights — small surcharge applies
  • Trolleybus 31, 33, 34Quiet, electric, overhead-wire routes through the city

Route numbers and schedules change occasionally — always confirm with the official planner before relying on this list.
