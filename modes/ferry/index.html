⛴️ Rhine ferries

Four small wooden boats, each tethered to a high cable, that cross the Rhine using only the river's own current. A Basel institution, not a commute.

What it is

The four Fähren are reaction ferries operated by the Basel Fähri-Verein, a non-profit. They have no engine: a tall cable spans the Rhine, and the ferryman angles the boat so the river's flow pushes it from one bank to the other. Each boat has a name, a coat of arms and a fixed crossing point:

  • Wild Maa — Klingental crossing.
  • Leu — by the Münster (cathedral).
  • Vogel Gryff — St. Alban crossing.
  • Ueli — St. Johann crossing, downstream.

When to ride, when to skip

Take a ferry when…

  • The day is sunny, you have ten minutes, and a bridge feels like the boring option.
  • You're walking the Rhine promenade and crossing earlier than the next bridge would force you.
  • You want a quintessential Basel experience that costs very little.

Skip a ferry when…

  • You're in a hurry — they only run when the river current and weather allow.
  • The Rhine is in flood, frozen, or the operator has suspended service. Always check faehri.ch.
  • It's late evening or a winter morning — operating hours are seasonal and shorter than the trams.
  • You're trying to use a TNW ticket — the ferries are not part of the fare network.

Tickets & apps

  • You pay the ferryman directly, in cash, on board. Modern card readers may or may not be present — assume cash and bring small coins.
  • Fares are deliberately modest; this is a community institution, not a commercial service.
  • The TNW network does not cover the ferries. A BaselCard does not get you a free crossing.
It's a piece of Basel folklore Three of the ferry names — Wild Maa, Vogel Gryff and Leu — are the three heraldic figures of the Vogel Gryff festival in Kleinbasel each January.
