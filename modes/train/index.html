🚆 Trains

Three stations, three operators, one of Europe's most distinctive rail crossroads.

What it is

Basel sits on the corner where Switzerland, Germany and France meet, and each country runs trains into the city.

  • Basel SBB — the main station, run by Swiss Federal Railways (SBB). All domestic Swiss long-distance, most international French (TGV Lyria), and Italian-bound services.
  • Basel Badischer Bahnhof ("Bad Bf") — the German station, technically on Swiss soil but operated by Deutsche Bahn (DB). ICEs to Frankfurt, Berlin and Hamburg call here.
  • Saint-Louis (Haut-Rhin) — the French regional station served by SNCF TER trains, just over the border.

Inside the region, the Basel S-Bahn runs frequent suburban services using SBB, DB and SNCF rolling stock under a coordinated schedule.

When to ride, when to skip

Take the train when…

  • You're going further than the tram or bus could reasonably take you (Liestal, Rheinfelden, Mulhouse, Lörrach, Zürich).
  • You want speed across the region — S-Bahn beats road traffic for almost any out-of-town trip.
  • You're arriving from another Swiss city or from abroad.

Skip the train when…

  • Your trip is entirely within the inner city — a tram is usually closer to where you actually want to be.
  • You'd need to backtrack to a station; the time saved by the train is eaten by the time spent walking to it.
  • Late at night, when intervals stretch and trams may be the better bet within Basel.

Tickets & apps

  • For trips within TNW zones, the same TNW ticket that's valid on tram and bus is also valid on the S-Bahn.
  • For trips beyond TNW (or for any long-distance journey), buy from SBB. The SBB Mobile app is excellent and sells tickets that include international segments.
  • Half Fare travelcard (Halbtax) is the most common Swiss frequent-traveller discount; the GA (Generalabonnement) covers all public transport in Switzerland.
  • For ICE trains via Basel Bad Bf, DB Navigator may have better ticket options than SBB for cross-border journeys.
Border quirk Basel SBB has a French section ("partie française"): platforms from which TER and TGV trains depart toward France. There's a passport-style border kiosk inside the station — bring your ID even if you're only popping over to Mulhouse.

Official resources

  • SBB CFF FFS Swiss Federal Railways. Tickets, journey planner, S-Bahn timetables.
  • Deutsche Bahn (DB) For ICEs from Basel Bad Bf to Frankfurt, Berlin, Hamburg.
  • SNCF French railways. TER and TGV services to Saint-Louis, Mulhouse, Paris.
  • TNW Regional fare network — covers S-Bahn within Basel zones.
