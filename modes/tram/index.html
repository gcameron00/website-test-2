🚊 Trams

The default way to get around Basel. Two operators, one ticket, and roughly 12 lines that cover the city centre and most suburbs.

What it is

Basel runs a dense electric tram network operated jointly by two companies: the city's own BVB (green liveried trams) and the canton's BLT (yellow liveried trams). They share tracks, share stops, and accept the same TNW ticket. From a passenger's point of view it's one network in two colours.

Trams are frequent — typically every 7–8 minutes on the trunk lines during the day — and almost all the rolling stock is low-floor and step-free.

When to ride, when to skip

Pick a tram when…

  • You're moving around the central city or inner suburbs.
  • You want frequent service without checking a timetable.
  • You have luggage, a stroller or a bike — low floors help.
  • You'd rather not navigate one-way streets and parking.

Skip the tram when…

  • You're going somewhere a tram doesn't reach — the hills, the outer villages, or directly to the airport (use a bus or train).
  • You're crossing the Rhine just for the experience of it (take a ferry instead).
  • You're going far out of town — an S-Bahn or InterCity train is faster.

Tickets & apps

  • A single TNW ticket is valid on every tram regardless of operator, plus buses and S-Bahn within the right zones.
  • Hotel guests usually receive a BaselCard at check-in that covers public transport in zones 10/11.
  • For day-trippers, a TNW day pass is almost always the right choice over single tickets.
  • Tickets can be bought at every stop's machine, or in the BVB / BLT apps. Conductors do check.
Cross-border curiosity BLT line 8 to Weil am Rhein and line 3 to Saint-Louis make Basel one of the few cities in the world with regular tram service into two different countries.
