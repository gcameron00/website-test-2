🚲 Bikes

Basel is flat, compact and threaded with bike lanes. For many short trips a bike is faster than a tram — and a lot more fun.

What it is

There are three main ways to get a bike for a trip in Basel, each suited to a different need.

  • PubliBike — Switzerland's national station-based bike-share. Pick up at one red docking station and return at another. App-driven.
  • Pick-e-Bike — free-floating e-bikes and e-scooters in Basel. No docking stations; end your rental anywhere in the service area.
  • Rent-a-Bike — daily/weekly rentals at Basel SBB and other train stations, aimed at tourers and day-trippers who want a quality bike for longer.

When to ride, when to skip

Take a bike when…

  • Your trip is between roughly 1 and 5 km — a bike usually wins door-to-door.
  • You're exploring along the Rhine or out to the countryside (the Hardwald, Petite Camargue Alsacienne, the Jura foothills).
  • You want flexibility to stop where you like.
  • The weather is decent — Basel drivers are generally cyclist-aware and infrastructure is good.

Skip the bike when…

  • You have heavy luggage or several small children — take a tram.
  • It's icy or snowing — Basel doesn't get much snow, but when it does, cycle paths aren't always cleared first.
  • You'd be locking up in a busy station for hours — bike theft is opportunistic in the city.
  • You need to cross long distances quickly — take a train.

Tickets & apps

  • Bikes are not covered by TNW or BaselCard — each operator has its own pricing and app.
  • PubliBike: per-minute or subscription rates via the PubliBike app.
  • Pick-e-Bike: per-minute via the Pick-e-Bike app; must be ended inside the service area.
  • Rent-a-Bike: book online or at a station counter; bring ID and a deposit. Best for full-day or multi-day use.
  • Bringing your own bike on the S-Bahn? You usually need a bike day pass (Velo-Tageskarte) on top of your normal ticket.
Helmet etiquette Helmets are not legally required for adults in Switzerland, but e-bikes that go above 25 km/h ("schnelle E-Bikes") do require a helmet and a yellow plate. Pick-e-Bike's bikes are the slower 25 km/h class.
