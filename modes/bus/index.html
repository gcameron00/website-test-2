🚌 Buses

The complement to the tram. Goes places trams can't — hills, residential suburbs, the airport, and over the borders.

What it is

Where the tram network ends, the bus network begins. Most local buses are operated by BVB and BLT alongside their trams. Routes that leave the cantonal area or run into rural Switzerland are typically run by Postauto, the yellow long-distance buses. International airport service to EuroAirport is run by BVB on its line 50.

When to ride, when to skip

Pick a bus when…

  • You're going to or from EuroAirport (BSL/MLH/EAP) — line 50 from SBB station is the standard option.
  • Your destination is on a hill or in an outer neighbourhood the trams skip (Bruderholz, Riehen Niederholz, parts of Allschwil and Bottmingen).
  • You're heading into rural cantons by Postauto.

Skip the bus when…

  • A tram covers the same route — the tram is usually faster and smoother.
  • You'd rather not transfer; some routes are infrequent evenings and Sundays.
  • You're prone to motion sickness — Basel's hilly suburbs and one-way streets make for twisty rides.

Tickets & apps

  • The same TNW ticket covers all local buses (BVB, BLT and Postauto in the relevant zones).
  • Cross-border routes — for example into Lörrach (DE) or Saint-Louis (FR) — may need a different ticket once you cross the fare zone boundary. Check the operator before boarding.
  • Buy at the stop machine, in the BVB / BLT apps, or via SBB Mobile.
Airport tip EuroAirport sits on French soil but has a Swiss exit. Bus line 50 from Basel SBB serves the Swiss exit — if you accidentally come out the French side you'll find buses run by French operators that go to Saint-Louis instead.
