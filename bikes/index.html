Bikes

Basel is mostly flat, has a dense network of bike lanes and a long cycling culture. For many short journeys, especially in good weather, a rental bike is the most pleasant way to travel.

Bike sharing

The two services most visitors will encounter are PubliBike (station-based, with both regular and electric bikes) and Donkey Republic (app-based, international). Both unlock with a smartphone app and let you return the bike at any station or designated parking zone.

  • PubliBikeStation-based, large network across Basel and Switzerland. Regular and e-bikes.
  • Donkey RepublicApp-only, dock at hubs across the city. Familiar for visitors from elsewhere in Europe.

Renting from a shop

For a longer rental, a higher-quality bike, or a child seat, renting from a local shop is usually a better experience. Several shops in Kleinbasel and around the SBB station rent by the day or week, with helmets and locks included.

When a bike is the right choice

  • You want to follow the Rhine path along both banks.
  • You're heading to a museum or park that's a 20-minute walk away.
  • The trams are crowded and the weather is on your side.
  • You're combining a city visit with a half-day ride into Alsace or the Tüllinger hill.

When a bike is the wrong choice

  • It's pouring rain, snowing or icy. (Trams keep running.)
  • You're carrying heavy luggage to the station.
  • You want to drink wine with dinner — Swiss bike rules treat alcohol seriously.
  • You're crossing the Rhine on a ferry — bring small change for the bike fare on board.

Rules of the road, briefly

  • Lights are required at night and in poor visibility.
  • Helmets are not legally required for adults but are common.
  • Use bike lanes where marked; share carefully with pedestrians where not.
  • Trams have right of way. Trams always have right of way.
Tram tracks and bike tyres do not get along. Cross them at as close to a right angle as you can manage.
