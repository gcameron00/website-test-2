Getting around Basel

Basel has one of the densest public transport networks in Europe for a city its size. Almost everywhere in town is reachable by tram or bus in under 20 minutes, the four Rhine ferries are powered by the river itself, and the SBB station is a 90-second walk from a tram that goes anywhere. This guide explains what each mode is good for — and points you to the official operators for tickets and timetables.

At a glance

A rough comparison of the five modes. Speed and cost are typical for a short in-city trip; check the operator for current fares.

Mode Best for Coverage Speed in city Ticket
Tram Everyday city trips City + inner suburbs Medium TNW
Bus Hills, suburbs, cross-border Wider than the tram Medium TNW
Ferry Crossing the Rhine for fun 4 fixed crossings Slow Cash on board
Train Out-of-town and intercity Region, Switzerland, EU Fast SBB / TNW for S-Bahn in zones
Bike Flexible, scenic, short trips Wherever you can pedal Fast for <3 km App subscription
One ticket for tram, bus and S-Bahn Most public transport in and around Basel is covered by the TNW fare network. A single TNW ticket is valid on BVB and BLT trams and buses, on Postauto, and on S-Bahn trains within the right zones — even when you switch operators mid-trip.

If you only read one thing

  • Staying in a hotel? Ask for your BaselCard at check-in — it gives you free public transport within the city for the duration of your stay.
  • Visiting for the day? A TNW day pass for zones 10/11 covers virtually everything you'll want to ride in the city.
  • Don't fight the apps. SBB Mobile handles trains and most journey planning; BVB and BLT have their own apps for tram/bus tickets and live departures.
