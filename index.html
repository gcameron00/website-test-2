Basel, Switzerland

Getting around Basel

Basel has one of the most comprehensive public transport networks in Switzerland. This site is a short, plain-language overview of your options — trams, buses, Rhine ferries, trains and bike rental — and the official apps and websites that will actually plan your journey.

Pick the right mode for the trip

Almost every journey inside Basel can be made on a single TNW ticket covering trams, buses and the S-Bahn. The four Rhine ferries are their own thing. Bikes are for when you want air and freedom.

Trams

The backbone of city transit. Frequent, electric, and they go almost everywhere you want to go.

Buses

Fill the gaps the trams don't reach — hills, outskirts, the airport, and weekend night routes.

Ferries

Four small reaction ferries cross the Rhine using only the current. Slow, charming, very Basel.

Trains

Three stations, three countries. Use SBB, S-Bahn or international rail for day trips and beyond.

Bikes

Rent for an hour or a day. Basel is largely flat, has bike lanes, and views along the Rhine are worth it.

Tickets & apps

One TNW ticket covers most local journeys. Here are the official apps you actually need.

Quick orientation

  • Local network operatorBVB (city) and BLT (regional), under the TNW fare union
  • Single ticket validityTrams, buses and S-Bahn within the bought zones
  • Free transport for hotel guestsMobility Ticket — given at check-in by registered hotels
  • Three main railway stationsBasel SBB, Basel Bad Bf (DB), Basel SNCF (within Basel SBB)

For anything time-sensitive — departures, disruptions, ticket purchase — go straight to the operator. The resources page lists the official apps and websites.
