Trams

The tram network is the backbone of Basel's public transport. If you're inside the city and not in a particular hurry, this is almost always the right answer.

What it is

Two operators run the Basel tram network: BVB (Basler Verkehrs-Betriebe — the green and yellow trams of the city of Basel) and BLT (Baselland Transport — yellow and red, reaching out into Baselland and the suburbs). They share the same tracks, stops and tickets, so as a passenger you can usually ignore the distinction.

Lines run frequently — typically every 7–8 minutes during the day on the busy routes — and the network covers most parts of the city. Tram 8 famously crosses into Weil am Rhein, Germany; tram 10 reaches Dornach in Solothurn. So a "city tram" can quietly take you to another country before lunch.

When to take a tram

  • Most journeys inside Basel city limits.
  • Hopping between the old town, the SBB station and the museums.
  • Travelling to nearby suburbs in Baselland (Riehen, Pratteln, Münchenstein, Bottmingen).
  • You want a step-free ride — most modern trams are low-floor.

When something else is better

  • You're going up into the hills or to the airport — take a bus.
  • You're crossing the Rhine and the bridges are a detour — try a ferry.
  • You're heading to Zürich, Bern or beyond — take the train.
